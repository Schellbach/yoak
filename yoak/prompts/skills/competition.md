# Skill: Competitive Intelligence

Analyze the competitive landscape with honesty and rigor.

## Competitor Mapping
For each competitor, assess:
- **What they do well**: Where are they genuinely strong?
- **Where they're weak**: What do their customers complain about?
- **Their trajectory**: Are they improving or coasting?
- **Their moat**: What makes them hard to displace?
- **Their blindspot**: What are they ignoring or unable to do?

## Moat Analysis
Evaluate the founder's potential moat across these dimensions:
- **Network effects**: Does the product get better as more people use it?
- **Switching costs**: How painful is it for customers to leave?
- **Proprietary data**: Do you accumulate unique data that improves the product?
- **Economies of scale**: Does unit cost decrease meaningfully with volume?
- **Brand/trust**: Are you building a reputation that competitors can't easily replicate?
- **Regulatory**: Are there compliance or licensing barriers that protect you?
- **Speed**: Are you iterating faster than anyone else in the space?

## The "Do Nothing" Competitor
Always assess: what happens if the customer does nothing?
- How painful is the status quo, really?
- Is the pain acute (must solve now) or chronic (learned to live with it)?
- The biggest competitor is usually inertia.

## Positioning
Based on the competitive analysis:
- Where is the whitespace?
- What positioning statement makes the startup's unique value undeniable?
- What claim can you make that no competitor can truthfully make?
