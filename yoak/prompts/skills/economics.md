# Skill: Unit Economics

Help the founder model and validate their unit economics.

## Core Metrics

### Customer Acquisition Cost (CAC)
- Total sales and marketing spend / number of new customers acquired.
- Include ALL costs: ads, content, sales team, tools, events.
- Calculate blended CAC and per-channel CAC.
- Track trend: is CAC increasing or decreasing over time?

### Lifetime Value (LTV)
- Average revenue per customer × average customer lifespan.
- For subscription: ARPU / monthly churn rate.
- Include gross margin in the calculation for accuracy.
- LTV should be calculated on a cohort basis.

### LTV/CAC Ratio
- Target: > 3:1 for a healthy business.
- < 1:1 means you're losing money on every customer.
- 1-3:1 means you might survive but it's tight.
- > 5:1 means you might be underinvesting in growth.

### Payback Period
- How many months until a customer's revenue covers their acquisition cost?
- Target: < 12 months for most businesses, < 18 months for enterprise.
- Shorter payback = faster reinvestment in growth.

## Burn Rate & Runway
- Monthly burn rate: total monthly expenses - total monthly revenue.
- Runway: cash in bank / monthly burn rate.
- Default alive or default dead? (PG) If growth and expenses stay constant, do you reach profitability before running out of cash?

## Revenue Model Validation
- Is pricing based on customer willingness to pay (evidence) or founder guesses?
- What pricing model fits? (subscription, transaction, freemium, per-outcome)
- What's the expansion revenue opportunity? (upsell, cross-sell)
- What does the pricing ladder look like?

## The "Default Alive" Test (Paul Graham)
Given your current revenue growth rate and current expenses:
- Will you reach profitability before running out of money?
- If yes: default alive. You control your destiny.
- If no: default dead. You need to either cut costs or increase growth.
- Every founder should know which they are.
