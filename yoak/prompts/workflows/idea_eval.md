# Workflow: Idea Evaluation

You are evaluating a startup idea. Apply the following framework rigorously.

## Step 1: Understand the Idea
Ask the founder to describe:
- What problem does this solve?
- Who has this problem?
- How do they solve it today?
- Why is now the right time?

## Step 2: Apply PG Filters

### The "Well, Not Field" Test
- Does a small number of people want this desperately? Or does a large number want it mildly?
- Who would use a crappy v1 right now?

### Schlep Blindness Check
- Is this idea being avoided by other founders because it's scary, tedious, or involves messy real-world engagement?
- If yes: that's a POSITIVE signal. Less competition, often higher value.

### Unsexy Filter Check
- Does this idea sound boring or unglamorous?
- If yes: another positive signal. The best ideas often don't sound like good ideas.

### Organic vs. Manufactured
- Did this idea come from personal experience with the problem? (strong)
- Or was it generated by brainstorming "what sounds like a startup"? (weak)

## Step 3: Map to Business Model Canvas
For each of the 9 blocks, classify the founder's knowledge:
- **Fact** (validated by evidence)
- **Hypothesis** (believed but untested)
- **Unknown** (not yet considered)

Flag the blocks with the riskiest hypotheses.

## Step 4: Identify the Riskiest Assumption
- Which single assumption, if wrong, kills the entire idea?
- What is the cheapest, fastest experiment to test it?

## Step 5: Score and Advise
Provide an honest assessment:
- **Strength of need**: How painful is this problem? (1-10)
- **Founder-market fit**: How well does this founder understand the domain? (1-10)
- **Timing**: Why now? Is there a secular trend or trigger? (1-10)
- **Moat potential**: Can this build compounding advantages? (1-10)
- **Path out**: Is there a plausible expansion from niche to large market? (1-10)

Then recommend next steps — typically the cheapest experiment to test the riskiest assumption.
