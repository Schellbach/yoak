# Workflow: Pivot Decision

You are helping the founder decide whether to pivot or persevere.

## Step 1: Evidence Review
Gather the data:
- What hypotheses have been tested?
- How many experiments have been run on the current thesis?
- What does the evidence say? (Summarize validated and invalidated hypotheses.)
- What does the learning journal show?

## Step 2: Pivot Signals Check

### Signals suggesting a pivot is needed:
- [ ] Consistently failing key milestones after 3+ iterations
- [ ] Customer interviews consistently invalidate a core hypothesis
- [ ] Users engage but don't convert or pay
- [ ] High acquisition cost with low retention
- [ ] Multiple experiments contradict the thesis
- [ ] Team morale declining because progress feels impossible
- [ ] Market has shifted (new competitor, regulation, technology change)

### Signals suggesting you should NOT pivot yet:
- [ ] Haven't rigorously tested the hypothesis (fewer than 3 significant iterations)
- [ ] Giving up after one or two negative data points
- [ ] Pivoting because the current work is boring/hard (not because data says so)
- [ ] A shiny new idea appeared but current thesis isn't exhausted
- [ ] Have paying customers and growing revenue, even if slow

## Step 3: Diagnosis
If pivoting seems right, identify:
- Which Business Model Canvas block(s) specifically failed?
- Is it the problem (wrong pain point), the customer (wrong segment), the solution (wrong approach), or the model (wrong economics)?

## Step 4: Pivot Type Selection
Based on the diagnosis, which pivot type fits?
- Zoom-in, Zoom-out, Customer Segment, Customer Need, Platform, Business Architecture, Value Capture, Engine of Growth, Channel, or Technology pivot.

## Step 5: New Hypothesis Formation
- Articulate the new hypothesis clearly.
- What evidence from previous experiments supports this new direction?
- What is the first experiment to run on the new thesis?
- Do you have enough runway to execute this pivot?

## Step 6: Record
Log the pivot in the learning journal:
- Date, what changed, why, what evidence triggered it, what the new hypothesis is.
