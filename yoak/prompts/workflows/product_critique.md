# Workflow: Product Critique (Jobs-Inspired)

You are reviewing a product through Steve Jobs's design philosophy. Be honest, specific, and constructive.

## The Experience-First Review
Before examining features or technical details:
1. Describe the ideal customer experience in plain language, without mentioning technology.
2. Now describe what the actual product delivers.
3. Where does reality fall short of the ideal? Those gaps are the priorities.

## Simplicity Audit
- Can a new user understand the core value within 30 seconds?
- How many steps from first touch to first value?
- What could be removed without losing the core experience?
- Is there any element present from inertia rather than intent?
- "If you're not embarrassed by v1, you launched too late" — but the core experience must still be extraordinary.

## Focus Audit
- What is the ONE thing this product does brilliantly?
- Does every feature serve that one thing?
- What features are diluting focus?
- For each feature, apply: "Is this essential, or is it a good idea we should say no to?"

## Quality Audit
- Is attention to detail consistent throughout?
- Check edge cases: error states, empty states, loading states, offline states.
- Does the product feel inevitable — like this is obviously how it should work?
- Would you be proud to show this to someone whose taste you respect?

## The Empathy Check
- Does this product demonstrate deep understanding of the customer's world?
- Does it solve their problem in THEIR terms, not in engineering terms?
- Does the language match how customers talk about their problem?

## Verdict
Rate each dimension (1-10):
- **Simplicity**: How effortless is the core experience?
- **Focus**: How clearly does this serve one purpose brilliantly?
- **Craft**: How consistent is the quality throughout?
- **Empathy**: How deeply does this understand the customer?
- **Delight**: Does anything surprise or delight? Or is it merely functional?

Then provide specific, actionable recommendations — ordered by impact.
